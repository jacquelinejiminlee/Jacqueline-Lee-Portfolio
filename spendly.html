01 — Overview

Banking, but built for the way we actually spend.

Concept

Spendly is a self-initiated banking concept aimed at college students and the broader 18–35 audience. The premise is simple: the apps we use to manage our money were built for a generation of users who paid for things alone. They don't fit the way our generation actually spends — splitting dinner four ways, tracking $4 coffees, comparing budgets with friends.

The project covers the full arc — identifying the problem, defining the audience, designing the brand identity, prototyping the core flows, and building out a social campaign that brings the brand to life.

02 — The Problem

Splitting the check is a hassle. So is figuring out where your money went.

Pain points

Picture a group of college students out for dinner. The bill comes — and most restaurants only let you split it two ways. So one friend pays the whole thing, and now everyone's squinting at a paper receipt trying to remember who ordered the salmon and who got the side of fries. Multiply that by every group dinner, every grocery run, every Uber.

Then on the other side: most banking apps only show you the merchant and the total. $87.43 at Trader Joe's. But what was actually in the bag? You don't know unless you kept the receipt.

03 — The Solution

Itemized receipts. Item-level splits. Friends in the loop.

Core idea

Spendly's core feature is item-level visibility. Every transaction in the app expands into a virtual receipt that mirrors the paper one — every drink, every appetizer, every grocery item, line by line. You see exactly where your money goes.

Once you can see the items, splitting becomes effortless. After one person pays the bill, they can tap individual line items and request the exact amount from any friend connected on the app — no more squinting at a thermal-printed receipt under restaurant lighting.

The pitch in one line
One person pays. Everyone splits by the item. The receipt knows who ordered what.

Layered on top: budget challenges with friends, a social feed that turns financial habits into something shared rather than isolating, and all your cards consolidated in one place.

04 — Audience

Designed for the generation that splits everything.

Who it's for

Gen Z and young millennials (18–35) — students, early-career professionals, anyone who lives with roommates, eats out with friends, and feels the friction of every group bill. They're financially conscious but not financially boring. They've already accepted that banking apps will be ugly. Spendly bets they'd rather it weren't.

05 — Brand Identity

Loud where banking is quiet. Pixel‑bright where banking is beige.

System

The identity leans into the contradiction at the heart of the product — financial software that doesn't take itself too seriously. Type pairs a pixelated display face (OffBit) for moments of personality with a clean grotesque (Dlea Gothic One) for impact and Inter for utility. Acid green and electric purple do the heavy lifting against deep black.

06 — Prototype

Every screen, walking the same line: financial, but never sterile.

UI

The home screen leads with a stack of cards — your main, your secondary, anything you've added — followed by quick send/receive actions and the active budget challenge from your friends. Tapping a transaction opens the full virtual receipt with itemized line entries. Long-press an item to request that exact amount from a connected friend. The challenges tab turns saving into something social — streaks, friends active, weekly goals.

07 — Campaign

Banking is boring. We fixed it.

Social rollout

The campaign extends Spendly past the app — a series of Instagram posts, billboards, and lifestyle photography built around three flat lines: See every single item. Split bills, zero drama. Budget with your crew. The visual system stays consistent: high-contrast type, acid green, friends mid-laugh, receipts mid-frame.
