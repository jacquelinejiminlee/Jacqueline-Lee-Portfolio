01 — Overview

Repositioning a Sunday ritual

Melrose Trading Post is a long-running outdoor marketplace in LA known for vintage vendors, independent creators, and a strong sense of local culture. While the market has a recognizable identity, it faces growing competition and shifting audience behaviors across social platforms.

With Trojan Marketing Group, I worked on brand strategy and social marketing planning — focusing on how MTP could strengthen its positioning and connect more clearly with Gen Z and young creative audiences. I designed the full pitch deck to translate research and insights into clear, engaging storytelling.

What I worked on

  • Audience research across locals, students, and tourists
  • Competitive landscape analysis of LA flea markets
  • Brand persona and strategic positioning framework
  • Campaign concept and content strategy
  • Full pitch deck design and visual direction

02 — MTP's Story

Grounding the brand in origin

The deck opens by establishing MTP's roots — founded in 1997 by Pierson Blaetz & Whitney Weston, hosting 260+ vendors weekly, and serving as both a cultural hub and a fundraiser for Fairfax High School arts programs. The brand story frames MTP not just as a market but as a community ritual.

03 — Competitive Landscape

Mapping the LA market

I benchmarked MTP against the four biggest flea markets in LA — Rose Bowl, Silverlake Flea, Long Beach Antique, and Artists & Flea — comparing price of entry, social following, philanthropy, and positioning.

MTP's competitive edge surfaced clearly: a sub-$6 entry, genuine philanthropy supporting Fairfax High and Greenway Arts Alliance, and a cultural identity that centers art and community rather than collectors or resale.

Key Insights

  • MTP has ~74K IG followers vs competitors at 127K–145K
  • Only MTP directly funds arts education & student jobs
  • Competitors lean collector, vintage, or resale — MTP owns community
  • TikTok presence is underdeveloped (~2K) — biggest growth lever

04 — Growth Goals

Where MTP could grow

Research showed MTP's reach was concentrated within LA and underperforming on TikTok. The strategy set concrete targets: grow Instagram from 74.3K to 80–85K, increase TikTok from 1.8K to 5K, raise engagement by 25%, and drive a 15% attendance lift through digital awareness.

05 — Audience Analysis

Three audiences,
one story

Research identified three audience tiers: locals (25–40) drawn by sustainability and live music, students (18–30, Gen Z) drawn by thrift culture and content creation, and tourists looking for authentic LA weekend experiences.

Rather than splitting the campaign, I identified a shared thread — the psychographic profile of an "authentic, expressive, treasure seeker" — that could unify messaging across all three segments.

Shared Psychographic

  • Authentic — values intentional, purpose-driven spending
  • Expressive — uses style & objects as identity
  • Treasure Seeker — loves the hunt for one-of-a-kind finds

07 — Persona & Insight

Meet Grace

To keep the strategy human, we built a brand persona: Grace, 23, a PR coordinator at LACMA who graduated from the University of Wisconsin and moved to LA a year ago. She loves hiking, trendy cafes, and volunteers at the LA Regional Food Bank. Homesick and eager to build community, she found MTP — and through it, a version of LA that finally feels like home.

Grace crystallized the strategic insight: as life moves online, people yearn for moments that feel real — face-to-face connection, self-discovery, and contributing to something larger than themselves.

08 — Strategy

The GET-TO-BY

All the research collapsed into one strategic sentence — the framework every campaign asset would ladder up to.

Get
Gen Z students and young Angelenos who crave authenticity and connection
To
spend their Sundays at Melrose Trading Post and see it as the cultural hub for real, creative community
By
showing that every visit is more than shopping — it's self-expression, discovery, and giving back to local arts

09 — Campaign

"Made at Melrose"

The final campaign translated strategy into three asset streams: short-form vendor spotlights to shift the theme from "Why MTP" to "Who makes MTP," a long-form documentary portraying MTP as LA's creative ecosystem, and a club collaboration with USC's Fashion Industry Association featuring Sunday Fit Checks, $20 challenges, and unique finds recaps.

Step 01 — Short Form

Meet the Vendors

Weekly spotlight on a different vendor, focusing on why they're a vendor and what they sell — shifting the narrative from "Why MTP" to "Who makes MTP."

Step 02 — Long Form

Creative Ecosystem

A documentary-style series on MTP's impact on LA's art, fashion, and small business — portraying Melrose as the cultural heartbeat of the city.

Step 03 — Club Collab

FIA at USC

A partnership with USC's Fashion Industry Association on Sunday Fit Checks, $20 Challenges, Unique Finds, and weekly Recaps to reach Gen Z directly.
