Marketing internship · Liner AI · Seoul

Making an AI research tool's value obvious in one scroll

Liner is a Seoul-based AI research tool company. As marketing intern, I worked across paid acquisition, influencer partnerships, and copy — all aimed at the same problem: translating a technical product into something a non-technical buyer immediately understood.

Problem

A powerful product that's hard to explain in an ad

Liner's core surfaces — a Search Agent API, Actions built on MCP, and a Model API — are genuinely technical. That's exactly what makes them hard to sell in a 15-second video or a single Instagram caption. Most of the team's existing acquisition content spoke fluently to other engineers, but the campaigns needed to reach a much wider, less technical audience across new international markets.

Solution

Three workstreams, one translation job

Rather than treating paid ads, influencer content, and copy as separate lanes, I ran them as one system: paid media to reach new markets, creators to build trust in professional niches, and copy that did the actual work of translating the tech into a plain-language reason to care. Each surface reinforced the same three value props, adapted to its format.

Research

Finding where the professionals already were

Rather than guessing at new markets, I looked at where Liner's existing technical audience already clustered — tech, AI, finance, and DevOps communities on LinkedIn, X, and Substack — and used that to prioritize which of six candidate countries to expand PMax targeting into first.

Testing & iteration

Rebuilding the ad creative until it actually converted

The first round of PMax creative for the browser extension and ChatGPT plugin under-performed against the core product's ads — early feedback made clear the value prop wasn't landing without the product's own context. I rebuilt the creative for each surface separately across static, 16:9, and 9:16 formats, testing which framing of "what this actually does for you" held attention longest before rolling the winning direction out across all seven markets.

Execution

Running the influencer program end to end

I sourced, negotiated, and briefed creators across LinkedIn, X, and Substack myself — owning the relationship from first outreach through payment on a managed monthly budget. Briefs and scripts translated the same three APIs (Search Agent, Actions/MCP, Model API) into demo posts a non-technical audience could follow, and creators delivered them across seven countries.
